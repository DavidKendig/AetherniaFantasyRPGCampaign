>"Brothers, sisters, comrades all, we answer to no king or hall! The fat lords feast while peasants starve; So we take what's ours… and then some more!"
> -- Common Recruitment Pitch

___
![[CrimsonThreadFlag.png]]
The Crimson Thread is the largest criminal syndicate in the known world. The Crimson Thread preaches that all wealth, land, and power in the world was stolen first by kings, nobles, merchants, and temples. Every castle was built on seized land. Every fortune was extracted from labor that was never fairly paid. Every title was invented by someone with a bigger army. Therefore nothing in the world is legitimately owned by anyone, and the Crimson Thread is simply taking back what was never rightfully given. This is the lie they tell themselves and the lie they tell recruits. It sounds reasonable for about five minutes until you watch them sell a farmer's daughter to a slaver and call it liberation from feudal dependence.

It is important to note that despite the surface level overlap the Crimson Thread is NOT a or the [Thieves Guild](Thieves%20Guild.md). The Thieves Guild has evolved a very antagonistic relationship with the Crimson Thread and views them as nothing more than thugs raveling in the misery of others via some mumbo jumbo creed.

The Crimson Thread is not dangerous just because they are strong. They are dangerous because they are *right about the problems of the world and monstrous about the solutions.* The lords are corrupt. The guilds exploit labor. The temples hoard wealth. The system is unfair. Every word of their critique of the power structure is true. And they use that truth as a doorway into atrocity.
___
## Organizational Ideology

The Crimson Thread's eschatological belief is that the current order is doomed and that a great leveling is coming, a day when all hierarchies will collapse and humanity will live as equals in a world without kings or gods. The Crimson Thread calls this "The Rising" and frames everything they do as hastening its arrival.

The Rising never comes, of course. It is always just around the corner. Just one more shipment, one more raid, one more territory seized and then the world will change. It is the perfect engine for endless expansion because the goal can never be reached.

The ideology functions on three levels within the organization:

**The True Believers** — mostly low-ranking members and recent recruits. These are people who were genuinely poor, genuinely oppressed, and genuinely angry. They heard the Thread's message and it made sense to them. They do the dangerous work, they die in raids, and they believe they are building something better. They are the most sympathetic and the most tragic members of the group.

**The Pragmatists** — mid-level operators who have figured out that the ideology is a useful tool. They don't necessarily believe in The Rising but they appreciate that it motivates the rank and file. They use the language when it's useful and ignore it when it isn't. They are in it for power, coin, and the thrill of operating above the law.

**The Inner Circle** — the leadership who crafted the ideology deliberately as a recruitment and control mechanism. They studied real revolutionary movements, real worker uprisings, and real philosophical texts and reverse-engineered a belief system designed to attract the desperate and justify the unjustifiable. Some of them may have once believed it. None of them do now. They live in luxury, command armies of fanatics, and have built exactly the kind of hierarchy they claim to oppose.

## Recruitment

The Crimson Thread recruits in places where their message lands hardest: famine-struck villages, debtor's prisons, workhouses, slums, and displaced refugee camps. A Crimson Thread recruiter shows up with food, speaks about how the system failed these people, and offers them a place in something bigger. By the time a recruit realizes what the Crimson Thread actually does, they are too deep to leave safely.

Each new member must go through an initiation that includes illegal activity (often an assault,
robbery, or major burglary). The Crimson Thread keeps evidence of the applicants’ guilt, which they use as leverage and insurance against future disloyalty.

If a recruit has a class, they will most likely be some level of rogue, but almost every class will be able to find a place in the Crimson Thread.

Barbarians, Fighters, and Rangers - serve as muscle for protection.
Bards - uses entertainment to coax information to find marks
Clerics - heal wounded bandits and magically enhance the abilities of fellow gang members.
Sorcerers and Wizards - use arcane magic to cover up or to facilitate their rackets

### Symbols and Language

The Crimson Thread uses a red cord or thread as their symbol, often worn as a bracelet or sewn into clothing. Members greet each other with code words, like *"The thread holds"* and respond with *"Until it binds the world"* to identify each other.

Over the years they have developed their own cant and euphemisms to conduct their business.  They genuinely use phrases like "redistributive action" for their raiding and "prospecting applicants" as they enslave with straight faces. These euphemisms are important as they allow members to discuss horrific acts without ever using words that might trigger their conscience.

## Organizational Structure 

The Crimson Thread is organized into a pyramid ~~(scheme)~~ with deliberately cryptic language at every level to help obfuscate their operations.
___
### The Weaver (The Big Boss)

Unknown to everyone but his (maybe her), lieutenants. Members call him The Weaver. He is the one who holds all the threads together. The title is spoken with reverence by the true believers, with respect by the pragmatists, and with careful neutrality by anyone smart enough to be afraid.

---
### The Needles (The Lieutenants)

The Crimson Thread has several underbosses called the Needles. Each Needle oversees a major arm of the Crimson Thread's operations. They answer only to the Weaver and they do not trust each other. A safeguard by the Weaver to help keep them from stabbing him in the back.

---

**Needle of the Harvest**

Commander of the Crimson Thread's piracy and raiding operations.

---

**Needle of the Binding**

Commander of the Crimson Thread's slave trading.

---

**Needle of the Awakening**

Commander of drug production and distribution for the Thread.

---

**Needle of the Cut**

Commander the Thread's assassination and wetwork division.

---

**Needle of the Gathering**

Commander of all theft, burglary, fencing, smuggling, and financial operations.

___
## The Spindles (Regional Commanders) 

Each Spindle manages operations in a city or significant territory and reports to the relevant Needle based on their primary function. A Spindle running a port might report to X, while one managing an urban drug network reports to Y. Some Spindles manage mixed operations and report to multiple Needles, which creates exactly the kind of internal tension the Crimson Thread wants.

___
## The Spools (Cell Leaders) 

Spools manage small teams of five to fifteen operatives. A Spool knows their team, their Spindle, and nothing else about the broader organization. This compartmentalization is deliberate, so a compromised Spool can only rat on their cell.

___
## The Threads (Rank & File Members)

Workers, soldiers, runners, lookouts, enforcers, pickpockets, sailors, cooks, guards. The bulk of the organization. They are told enough ideology to stay motivated and nothing about the structure above their Spool. 

___
## The Reds (New Recruits)

For Reds, the name is more of a demeaning joke than an actual title within the Crimson Thread. They are often called Red Shirts, as a common task within their trial period is often basic thuggery. They do the worst jobs, receive the least pay, and are watched constantly. Roughly half wash out, flee, or are quietly disposed of if they show signs of disloyalty.

___
## Internal Practices

**The Red Meeting** — a monthly gathering where Threads in a cell reaffirm their commitment to The Rising. Part loyalty check, part group therapy, part religious service. Members confess doubts, share grievances against the old system, and receive new doctrinal teachings. It is surprisingly effective at maintaining cohesion because it gives members a sense of community that most of them have never had before.

**The Fraying** — the Thread's term for punishment of members who break rules or show disloyalty. Minor infractions result in demotion and shunning. Major infractions result in being "cut from the Thread" — which is a euphemism for execution, though occasionally a valuable member is instead mutilated and released as a warning. A member who has been Frayed and survived always loses a finger. The specific finger indicates the crime.

**The Loom** — the Thread's intelligence network. Not a separate division but a function that runs through all levels. Every member is expected to report useful information upward. Spools compile reports for Spindles, Spindles compile for Needles, and Needles compile for the Weaver. The result is that the Weaver has an enormous amount of information about everything happening in his territory, though the quality varies wildly.

**The Tithe** — all revenue flows upward. Threads keep a small allowance. Spools take a percentage. Spindles take a larger percentage. Needles take whatever they want. The Weaver takes whatever is left. The system is presented as communal sharing but it is functionally identical to taxation by a feudal lord, which is an irony lost on no one except the true believers.

## Gather Information About the Crimson Thread

DC 10: The Crimson Thread run all organized crime in the city, from burglary to muggings to protection rackets.

DC 15: The gang also runs a thriving black market and smuggling operation. It has a near-monopoly on the trade in illicit goods.

DC 20: A mysterious man called the Spindle runs the Crimson Thread. Their main hideout is somewhere under the city, accessed through the storm sewer system.

If the PCs are looking for illicit goods, a DC 15 Gather Information check is sufficient to put them in contact with the Crimson Thread for a bargaining session.
