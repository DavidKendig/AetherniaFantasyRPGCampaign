## Overview

**The Condottieri** is one of the largest and most established guilds in the Empire. What began as a wartime coalition of sellsword companies during the Saints' War has, over the centuries since that conflict's end, evolved into something closer to a professional guild of adventurers, problem-solvers, and hired specialists. Any citizen of the Empire may approach the Condottieri to commission a contract, and the guild will assemble a **free company**, a hand-picked team of licensed contractors, to see it done.

The guild operates under Imperial charter, which grants it the legal authority to broker contracts, arm its members, and operate across realm borders without requiring separate permissions from each of the Twelve Realms. In exchange, the Condottieri is bound to accept only lawful contracts, submit to Imperial audit, and refuse work that would destabilize the Empire or violate the Emperor's peace.

## History

### Origins in the Saints' War

The Saints' War was a conflict of shattering scale -- a holy war that drew in nations, faiths, and powers from across the known world. The details of its causes and conclusion are recorded elsewhere, but its relevance to the Condottieri is this: the war created an enormous demand for soldiers that no single nation could fill.

Mercenary companies flooded the conflict from every corner of the Empire and beyond. Some were professional outfits with long histories. Others were little more than armed brigands who saw opportunity in the chaos. They fought on every side, switched allegiances when the coin was better, and left a trail of devastation that rivaled anything the regular armies managed.

Among these companies, a handful distinguished themselves -- not just for their fighting ability, but for their reliability. Companies that honored their contracts, protected civilians when they could, and refused to engage in the worst excesses of the war. These outfits, through a combination of pragmatism and genuine principle, began coordinating with one another. They shared intelligence, negotiated collectively, and established informal codes of conduct that their members were expected to follow.

By the war's final years, this loose coalition had a name: **the Condottieri**, from an old word meaning "those who are contracted." Their leader was a former siege engineer named **Aldric Venturro**, who had the organizational mind of a quartermaster and the political instincts of a diplomat. When the Saints' War finally ground to its bloody conclusion, Venturro saw what was coming: tens of thousands of trained soldiers with no war to fight, no farms to return to, and no one willing to pay them.

### The Charter of 0089 AE

Rather than allow the demobilized mercenary companies to become the roving bandit armies that everyone feared, Venturro petitioned the Imperial throne for a charter. His proposal was simple: formalize the mercenary trade. License companies. Regulate contracts. Create a structure that channeled the skills of former soldiers into legitimate work -- guarding caravans, clearing monster-infested roads, resolving disputes that fell below the threshold of military intervention but above the capacity of local constabularies.

The Emperor -- who had just survived a war and had no appetite for another one fought by his own unpaid veterans -- agreed. In **0089 AE**, the Imperial Charter of the Condottieri was signed, granting the guild legal standing, the right to bear arms under contract, and jurisdiction across all Twelve Realms.

Venturro became the first **Grandmaster**, and the mercenary contractors of the Saints' War became the founding members of what would grow into the Empire's most ubiquitous guild.

### Evolution Over the Centuries

The Condottieri of the present day bears only a passing resemblance to the post-war mercenary coalition of Venturro's time. Over the centuries, the guild has broadened dramatically. While martial contracts -- monster hunting, caravan escort, bodyguard work, bounty recovery -- remain the bread and butter of the organization, the modern Condottieri handles an enormous range of commissions:

- **Exploration and Survey** -- Mapping uncharted territory, scouting ruins, surveying land for settlement or resource extraction
- **Investigation** -- Tracking down missing persons, uncovering fraud, solving crimes that overwhelm local authorities
- **Diplomatic Escort** -- Providing neutral security for sensitive negotiations between realms or noble houses
- **Specialist Retrieval** -- Recovering stolen goods, lost artifacts, or sensitive documents
- **Pest and Threat Clearance** -- Eliminating dangerous creatures, clearing Wyrd-tainted areas, dealing with supernatural disturbances
- **Infrastructure Support** -- Protecting construction crews in dangerous territory, escorting engineers, guarding supply lines

The guild's motto -- *"Name the work"* -- reflects this breadth. If it is legal, and a price can be agreed upon, the Condottieri will find someone to do it.

## Structure and Organization

### The Grand Charter Hall

The Condottieri is governed from its headquarters, the **Grand Charter Hall**, a sprawling complex in Encara that serves as the guild's administrative center, primary archive, and political seat. The building was originally a military barracks constructed during the Saints' War and has been expanded continuously ever since, resulting in an architectural style best described as "functional accretion."

### Leadership

| Rank | Title | Role |
|------|-------|------|
| 1 | **The Grandmaster** | Supreme authority of the guild. Elected by the Council of Captains for a ten-year term, renewable once. Represents the guild before the Emperor and the Landsrad. |
| 2 | **The Council of Captains** (12 members) | Senior governing body. One Captain represents each of the Twelve Realms, elected by the guild members operating within that realm. Sets guild policy, adjudicates disputes, and approves high-value contracts. |
| 3 | **Marshals** | Regional administrators who oversee guild operations within a specific territory. Responsible for maintaining charter halls, reviewing contracts, and disciplining members. |
| 4 | **Charter Masters** | Experienced administrators who run individual charter halls. They receive contract requests, assess them, and match them with appropriate free companies. The face of the guild for most civilians. |
| 5 | **Captains** | Leaders of established free companies. A Captain is an experienced contractor who has earned the right to recruit, lead, and vouch for their own company. |
| 6 | **Contractors** | Fully licensed guild members. They have completed their apprenticeship, passed the guild's assessment, and are authorized to accept contracts independently or as part of a free company. |
| 7 | **Apprentices** | Prospective members serving a probationary period under the supervision of a licensed Contractor or Captain. |

### Charter Halls

The guild maintains **charter halls** in every major city and most significant towns across the Twelve Realms. These halls serve as the primary interface between the guild and the public. A charter hall is part office, part tavern, part armory, and part courthouse. Citizens come here to post contracts, contractors come here to find work, and disputes between the two are settled here under guild law.

Each charter hall prominently displays a **contract board** -- a large notice board where available contracts are posted, ranked by difficulty, compensation, and urgency. The sight of a crowd of armed contractors studying a contract board has become one of the most recognizable scenes of daily life in the Empire's cities.

## Free Companies

### What Is a Free Company?

A **free company** is the basic operational unit of the Condottieri -- a team of contractors assembled to fulfill a specific contract. Free companies range in size from as few as three members to as many as several dozen, depending on the scope of the work.

Some free companies are **standing** -- permanent teams that work together contract after contract, developing the kind of trust and coordination that only comes from shared hardship. These companies have their own names, reputations, and traditions. The best-known standing companies are famous across the Empire, and competition for membership in them is fierce.

Other free companies are **provisional** -- assembled by a Charter Master for a specific contract and dissolved upon its completion. Provisional companies are more common for routine work, while standing companies tend to take on the more dangerous, complex, or prestigious contracts.

### Formation and Composition

A standing free company is formed when a licensed Captain files a **Company Charter** with the guild, declaring the company's name, its operating territory, and its core membership. The Captain is personally responsible for the conduct of their company and stakes their reputation -- and their guild standing -- on every contract their people accept.

Free companies are deliberately diverse in composition. The Condottieri learned long ago that a team of nothing but swordsmen is useless when the contract requires picking a lock, navigating a swamp, or negotiating with a hostile noble. A well-composed free company might include:

- **Combat specialists** -- Fighters, archers, duelists, former soldiers
- **Scouts and rangers** -- Trackers, survivalists, pathfinders
- **Specialists** -- Locksmiths, trap-disablers, siege engineers, demolition experts
- **Scholars and loremasters** -- Historians, linguists, cartographers, appraisers
- **Healers** -- Field medics, herbalists, or clergy with healing training
- **Mystics** -- Those with arcane or divine training, when the contract demands it

The best Captains know that the right team for the job is worth more than the strongest team available.

### Notable Standing Companies

A few of the Empire's best-known free companies include:

- **The Ironbound** -- One of the oldest standing companies, tracing its lineage to the original Saints' War coalition. Specializes in monster hunting and Wyrd-tainted threat clearance. Their reputation for reliability is matched only by their reputation for charging accordingly.

- **The Crimson Ledger** -- An investigation-focused company known for solving cases that baffle local authorities. Their founder, a former Imperial magistrate, insisted that every member be trained in law, evidence handling, and interrogation. They are meticulous, expensive, and almost never wrong.

- **The Wayfarers** -- A large, loosely organized company that specializes in exploration and survey contracts in the Empire's frontier regions. They have mapped more uncharted territory than any other single organization and maintain an extensive private archive of maps and geographic data.

- **The Last Light** -- A small, elite company that takes contracts exclusively related to Wyrd corruption and supernatural threats. Several of its members are former Argent Tide knights who left the order for reasons they decline to discuss. They are very good at what they do, and what they do is very dangerous.

## The Contract System

### How Contracts Work

The contract system is the backbone of the Condottieri's operations. It works as follows:

1. **Posting** -- A client approaches a charter hall and describes the work they need done. The Charter Master helps them formalize it into a **contract writ** -- a legal document specifying the scope of work, the deadline, the compensation offered, and any special conditions.

2. **Assessment** -- The Charter Master assigns the contract a **tier rating** based on estimated difficulty and danger:

| Tier | Name | Description |
|------|------|-------------|
| I | **Errand** | Low-risk, routine work. Delivery, simple escort, minor pest removal. Suitable for apprentices or solo contractors. |
| II | **Commission** | Moderate difficulty. Caravan guard duty, investigation, bounty recovery. Standard free company work. |
| III | **Charter** | Significant danger or complexity. Monster hunting, ruin exploration, politically sensitive operations. Requires an experienced company. |
| IV | **Warrant** | High danger. Major threats, extended operations in hostile territory, contracts involving Wyrd corruption. Only veteran companies with proven records need apply. |
| V | **Grand Warrant** | Exceptional, empire-level significance. Rarely issued. Requires Council of Captains approval. The kind of contract that makes or breaks reputations -- and occasionally makes widows. |

3. **Acceptance** -- The contract is posted on the charter hall's contract board. Interested contractors or free companies review it and bid for the work. For higher-tier contracts, the Charter Master may directly approach companies they believe are suited to the task.

4. **Execution** -- The company carries out the contract. They are bound by guild law to complete the work as specified, or to report honestly if conditions on the ground make completion impossible.

5. **Completion and Payment** -- Upon verified completion, the client pays the agreed fee. The guild takes a **fifteen percent commission** on all contracts brokered through its halls, which funds guild operations, maintains charter halls, and supports the pension system for retired members.

### Contract Law

The guild enforces its contracts with the full weight of Imperial charter. Key principles include:

- **No Unlawful Contracts** -- The Condottieri will not broker contracts that violate Imperial law. No assassinations, no theft, no suppression of legal rights, no work that would constitute treason or rebellion. Contractors caught accepting off-book illegal work face expulsion and criminal prosecution.

- **Completion Obligation** -- Once accepted, a contract must be completed or formally abandoned with cause. Walking away from a contract without justification results in fines, suspension, or expulsion.

- **Client Protection** -- Contractors may not extort, threaten, or defraud their clients. The guild's reputation depends on civilian trust, and violators are dealt with harshly.

- **Contractor Protection** -- Clients who misrepresent the danger, scope, or conditions of a contract are liable for additional compensation and may be barred from future guild services. The Condottieri protects its own.

- **The Neutrality Clause** -- Free companies operating under guild contract are considered politically neutral. They may not be conscripted, detained, or taxed by local authorities while under active contract. This protection is one of the guild's most jealously guarded privileges and has been tested in court more times than anyone cares to count.

## Membership and Advancement

### Becoming a Contractor

The path to guild membership follows a straightforward but demanding process:

1. **Application** -- Any person of legal age may apply at a charter hall. There are no restrictions by birth, race, sex, or social class. The Condottieri was founded by commoners and has never pretended otherwise.

2. **Assessment** -- Applicants undergo an evaluation of their skills, fitness, and temperament. The guild is not looking for the strongest fighter or the cleverest scholar -- it is looking for people who can work in a team, follow instructions, keep their nerve under pressure, and resist the temptation to cut corners when no one is watching.

3. **Apprenticeship** -- Accepted applicants serve as apprentices for a minimum of one year, working under the supervision of a licensed Contractor or Captain. Apprentices accompany free companies on contracts, handle support tasks, and learn the guild's codes, customs, and operational standards.

4. **Licensing** -- After completing their apprenticeship and receiving endorsement from their supervising Contractor, apprentices sit a **licensing examination** administered by a Marshal. The exam tests practical skills, knowledge of guild law and contract procedure, and -- critically -- judgment. The guild has no use for talented idiots.

5. **Full Membership** -- Licensed Contractors receive a **guild seal** -- a stamped iron token bearing their name, license number, and the Condottieri's emblem. This seal is their proof of standing and is required to accept contracts through guild halls.

### The Captain's Writ

Contractors who wish to form their own standing free company must petition for a **Captain's Writ**. This requires:

- A minimum of five years as a licensed Contractor
- A record of successful contract completions with no major infractions
- Endorsement by at least two sitting Marshals
- A filed Company Charter specifying the company's name, founding members, and operational focus
- A bond of surety deposited with the guild, guaranteeing the Captain's financial responsibility for their company's conduct

The Captain's Writ is not given lightly. A bad Captain can destroy a company's reputation -- and by extension, the guild's.

## Culture and Traditions

### The Contractor's Code

While not a formal legal document, the **Contractor's Code** is an informal set of principles that most guild members live by. It is never written down in full -- it is passed from veterans to apprentices through example, correction, and the occasional sharp word over drinks. Its core tenets are widely understood:

- **Honor the contract.** Your word is your bond. Break it, and you are nothing.
- **Know the job before you take it.** Surprises get people killed.
- **Protect your company.** The person beside you matters more than the person paying you.
- **Leave the place better than you found it.** You are not raiders. Act accordingly.
- **Get paid.** Charity is for temples. You have a trade. Respect it.

### The Toast of the Fallen

At the conclusion of every contract in which a member of the company was killed, the surviving members gather and pour a measure of drink onto the ground before drinking their own. The Captain speaks the fallen's name and says: *"They took the contract. They held the line. The ledger is balanced."* It is a simple tradition, but an old one, and even the most hardened veterans observe it without irony.

### Guild Marks

Veteran contractors often bear **guild marks** -- small tattoos or brands that record their service. Common marks include:

- A **tally mark** on the forearm for every year of licensed service
- A **crossed swords** mark for surviving a Tier IV or V contract
- A **broken chain** mark for a contract that went catastrophically wrong but from which the contractor survived
- The **empty cup** -- the mark of a contractor who has lost a company member. It is considered disrespectful to ask about it unless invited to do so

### The Grandmaster's Feast

Once a year, on the anniversary of the guild's founding, the Grandmaster hosts a feast at the Grand Charter Hall in Encara. Every contractor in the city is invited, and charter halls across the Empire hold their own local celebrations. It is the one day of the year when guild hierarchy is officially suspended -- apprentices drink with Marshals, and Captains serve food to their own company members. The feast traditionally concludes with the Grandmaster reading the names of every contractor who died on contract during the preceding year.

## Relationship with the Empire

The Condottieri occupies a unique position in the Imperial power structure. It is not a branch of the government, but it operates under Imperial charter and is subject to Imperial law. It is not a military force, but its members are authorized to bear arms and use lethal force under contract. It is not a noble institution, but it holds the 13th seat on the Landsrad -- a vote that gives the guild direct influence over Imperial legislation.

This position generates both privilege and friction. The Twelve Realms tolerate the Condottieri because it provides a service they need -- a flexible, professional workforce that can handle problems too small for armies and too dangerous for constables. But local lords frequently chafe at the guild's cross-border privileges, and the Neutrality Clause has sparked more than one diplomatic incident when a free company's contract put it at odds with a realm's local interests.

The Emperor, for the most part, values the guild as a stabilizing force. Idle veterans cause rebellions. Busy veterans cause paperwork. The Imperial throne has consistently preferred the latter.

## Reputation and Public Perception

The Condottieri is one of the most visible institutions in the Empire, and public opinion of it varies widely:

- **Common folk** generally view the guild favorably. When your village is threatened by bandits or beasts and the local lord's garrison is three days away, the charter hall is where you go. The guild's willingness to take any lawful contract, no matter how modest, has earned it deep goodwill among ordinary citizens.

- **The nobility** has a more complicated relationship with the guild. Many lords rely on free companies for work their own forces cannot or will not do. Others resent the guild's independence, its cross-border authority, and the uncomfortable implication that the Empire's chartered adventurers can solve problems that its hereditary rulers cannot.

- **The military** regards the Condottieri with professional respect tempered by institutional rivalry. Imperial officers acknowledge the guild's competence but bristle at the suggestion that contractors are interchangeable with soldiers. The two institutions cooperate when necessity demands it and avoid each other when it does not.

- **The Synod of the Myst** has an official position of neutrality toward the guild but maintains quiet concerns about free companies that operate in Wyrd-tainted areas without proper spiritual supervision. The occasional friction between Condottieri contractors and Argent Tide knights over jurisdiction in supernatural matters is a recurring headache for both organizations.

## Summary

The Condottieri is the Empire's answer to an ancient problem: what do you do with skilled, dangerous people when the war is over? Aldric Venturro's answer -- give them a charter, a code, and a contract board -- has endured for centuries because it works. The guild is imperfect, occasionally corrupt, and perpetually entangled in the politics of the Twelve Realms. But it fills a gap that no other institution can, and the Empire would be a more dangerous place without it.

For the contractors themselves, the guild is something simpler: a way to make a living doing hard work in a hard world, with people they trust, under rules they understand.

Name the work. They will get it done.
