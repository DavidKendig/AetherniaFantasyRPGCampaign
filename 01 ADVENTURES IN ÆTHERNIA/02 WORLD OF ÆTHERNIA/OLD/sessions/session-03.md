# Session 3: What the Ruins Remember

[< Back to Campaign Index](../README.md) | [Previous: Session 2](session-02.md)

## Session Overview

**Goal:** The players enter the Thornwood and find the first real evidence that the world is not what they thought. This is the transition from "local mystery" to "something much larger." Still low fantasy — but the cracks are showing.

**Tone:** Exploration. Discovery. Awe mixed with dread.

**Estimated Length:** 4-5 hours (this is a bigger session)

---

## Part 1: Into the Thornwood

### Preparation
Maren provides the players with:
- A rough sketch from her grandmother's journals showing a landmark — "a clearing where three oaks grow from one root" — marking the path to the pale stones
- Bundles of dried sage and iron filings. "My grandmother's journals say iron wards against the old things. I don't know if that's superstition. Bring it anyway."
- A request: bring back rubbings of any carvings they find, and *do not touch the pale stones themselves*.

The Reeve won't sponsor this trip. Dalla Voss isn't available (she's returned to Dunmore). The players are on their own.

### The Journey

**The Thornwood Edge (1 hour in):**
- Normal enough. Old growth forest. Timber cutting trails.
- Players may encounter woodcutters who warn them not to go deeper. "The deep wood is off. Has been for weeks."

**The Deep Thornwood (2-3 hours in):**
The forest changes. Not dramatically — subtly.
- The air is warmer than it should be
- Sound behaves oddly: voices carry too far, footsteps echo
- No birdsong. No insects. Absolute silence except for the players and the wind.
- Trees are older here — massively old. Trunks wider than a house.
- Some trees have growths on them: geometric bulges in the bark that look almost carved. They are warm to the touch.
- Navigation is difficult. Paths seem to curve. The forest resists straight lines. Players with good navigation skills can push through; others may need to rely on the landmark Maren described.

**The Three-Root Oak:**
- Exactly as described in the journals. Three enormous oaks growing from a single root system in a small clearing.
- The root is exposed and partially petrified — turned to stone over centuries. It too is warm.
- Beyond the clearing, the forest opens slightly. The trees pull back as if repelled by something ahead.

## Part 2: The Pale Stones

### The Clearing

A wide, roughly circular clearing. The ground is bare — no grass, no undergrowth. Just dark, packed earth. In the center:

**Seven white stones** arranged in a circle, each roughly the height of a person. They are smooth, featureless, and so pale they seem to glow faintly even in daylight. They are not carved — they appear to be natural formations, but the arrangement is too perfect to be coincidence.

This is one of the seven **Veilstones** — an anchor point for the [Veil](../world/history.md). The players don't know that yet.

### What the Players Experience

**Sight:**
- The stones pulse with a faint, barely-perceptible light — like a heartbeat
- The air between the stones shimmers very slightly, like heat haze
- If players look at the stones from different angles, they seem *taller* or *shorter* depending on where they stand, though they don't actually change

**Sound:**
- The hum from the bridge is here, but stronger. Not painful — resonant. It fills the body.
- If players listen carefully, the hum has a *pattern*. Not music. Not language. Something older.

**Touch:**
- The stones are warm — warmer than anything else they've encountered
- Touching a stone produces a flash of sensation: the dream vision, but waking. The pale light, the sense of something vast underground, the sound that is almost a voice. It lasts two heartbeats and fades.
- **Iron reacts.** Any iron brought near the stones vibrates faintly. Maren's iron filings scatter in patterns on the ground near the stones — radiating outward like iron filings around a magnet.

**Feeling:**
- An overwhelming sense of *presence*. Not a creature — a place. The clearing feels alive.
- Some players may feel drawn to the stones. Others may feel pushed away. Both are valid reactions to ambient magical energy.

### Investigation Findings

1. **Carvings at the base:** Each stone has carvings at its base, hidden by dirt and age. The same old script as the mill and the buried stones. These are more extensive — paragraphs of text, not just markers.

2. **The ground between the stones:** If players dig even a few inches between the stones, they find a stone floor — a buried platform or foundation. The Veilstone circle sits on top of something.

3. **The line continues:** The alignment from Millhaven (mill, buried stone, bridge) terminates here. The Pale Stones are the destination.

4. **One stone is cracked.** The northernmost stone has a hairline fracture running from its base to about halfway up. It is the only imperfection. The crack is recent (clean edges, no weathering). This stone is slightly dimmer than the others.

   > **GM Note:** This crack is a resonance effect from the first Veilstone's destruction in the Ashfall Peaks. The Veilstones are linked. When one was destroyed, the others were stressed. This one is the weakest — and it's why [Seraine](../npcs/seraine-duskhollow.md) will come for it next.

5. **No hostiles.** Nothing attacks the players here. This place is sacred, ancient, and sleeping. The danger is not what's here — it's what will happen if this place is disturbed.

## Part 3: The Return

### The Walk Back
- The forest is easier to navigate going out than coming in (the resistance was directional — keeping things out of the clearing)
- Players may notice the forest seems *slightly* more alive on the way back. A bird singing where there was silence before. A fox watching them from a distance. The Thornwood is waking up, just a little.

### Reporting Back

**Maren Blackthorn:**
When shown the rubbings from the Pale Stones, Maren goes pale. She compares them to her grandmother's journals and says:

> "My grandmother called these *anchor marks*. She said they hold the world in place. She said there were seven places like this, and if they ever broke..." She stops. "I thought she was mad. These are real. You've been there."

She doesn't know what the anchors hold. She doesn't know about the Veil. But she knows this is bigger than Millhaven.

**Reeve Ashford:**
If told, he doesn't want to hear it. Old stones in the forest are not his problem. Harvests and taxes are his problem. He is not a bad man — he is a small man in a role that is about to become much too large.

**Brennan Cask:**
If players tell him about the Pale Stones, he gets very quiet. Then he says:

> "When I was a caravan guard, we went through the Ashfall Peaks once. There was a place up there — a mine the locals called the Hollows. My captain said to stay away from it. Said the air tasted wrong. Like metal and honey." He pauses. "Same as you're describing."

This is the first breadcrumb connecting Millhaven's events to the Ashfall Peaks — and the first destroyed Veilstone.

## End of Session

### What the Players Know
- There are ancient structures and stones beneath and around Millhaven
- The Pale Stones in the Thornwood are the focal point
- The strange events (rot, dreams, warmth) radiate from or connect to these stones
- The carvings are in an unknown ancient script
- Iron interacts strangely with whatever energy is at work
- One stone is cracked — and that seems bad
- Brennan's story suggests a similar place exists far to the north

### What the Players Don't Know (Yet)
- What the Veil is
- Who Seraine is
- That the Ashfall Peaks site has already been destroyed
- That the crack in the stone is a warning of what comes next

### Where This Goes
The players now have a genuine mystery with leads pointing in multiple directions:
- **The carvings** — someone must be able to read them. Perhaps a scholar in Dunmore or Valdris City.
- **The Ashfall Peaks** — Brennan's story. What happened at the Hollows?
- **Maren's journals** — more to decode. She'll work on it between sessions.
- **The cracked stone** — it's getting worse. The players may not know it yet, but they've just found something worth protecting.

## Loot & Rewards
- No direct payment (unless the Reeve is persuaded to compensate them — unlikely)
- Significant lore advancement
- Maren as a committed ally
- Brennan as a source of previously-hidden knowledge
- The beginning of a real quest — not a contract, but a calling
