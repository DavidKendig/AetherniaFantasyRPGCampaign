# The Crisis: The Fraying

[< Back to Campaign Index](../README.md) | [Main Arc](main-arc.md)

## What Is Happening

The [Veil](../world/history.md) — the metaphysical barrier separating the mortal world from the Otherworld — is being deliberately weakened. [Seraine Duskhollow](../npcs/seraine-duskhollow.md) destroyed the first of seven Veilstones six months before the campaign begins. The effects are spreading outward like cracks in ice.

The locals call the strange events many things. Scholars don't have a name for it yet. The GM can call it **The Fraying**.

## How the Fraying Manifests

The Fraying is not an invasion or a plague. It is the world *remembering what it used to be*. Magic is seeping back through the cracks, but it has been absent so long that it manifests *wrong* — twisted, confused, and unsettling.

### Tier 1: Whispers (Current — Sessions 1-3)
These are subtle. Most people will explain them away.

- Grain rots in unusual patterns — as if something is feeding on it from the inside
- Animals behave strangely: livestock staring at nothing, birds flying in unnatural formations, insects avoiding certain areas entirely
- People have vivid, shared nightmares — the same images recurring across unrelated dreamers
- Old stone structures (bridges, foundations, ruins) occasionally feel warm to the touch
- Milk sours faster. Iron tarnishes overnight. Small, dismissable things.
- A persistent, faint smell of ozone near the Old Stone Bridge in [Millhaven](../locations/millhaven.md)

### Tier 2: Tremors (Sessions 4-8)
These cannot be easily dismissed.

- Crops growing impossibly fast — or in impossible shapes (wheat spiraling, roots forming patterns)
- Animals born with mutations: extra eyes, wrong proportions, unusual intelligence
- People in affected areas begin manifesting minor abilities: sensing emotions, moving small objects, seeing things others can't
- Old ruins begin to glow faintly at night
- Weather anomalies: localized fog, impossible frost in summer, rain that falls upward
- The Thornwood becomes hostile — paths shift, sounds echo wrongly, travelers get lost in familiar areas

### Tier 3: Fractures (Sessions 9-15)
The world is visibly changing.

- Floating stones. Water flowing uphill. Gravity fluctuations near Veilstone sites.
- Spirits become visible — translucent figures in old places, voices on the wind
- Some people develop significant magical abilities. Others are driven mad by the change.
- The landscape physically transforms near destroyed Veilstones: impossible geometry, bioluminescent growth, areas where time moves differently
- Creatures from the Otherworld begin to cross over — small ones at first, then larger

### Tier 4: The Flood (Sessions 16+)
Full high fantasy. The Veil is critically weakened.

- Magic is everywhere. The world is unrecognizable in places.
- The Otherworld and the mortal world are bleeding together
- Massive supernatural entities become aware of the mortal world
- Everything the players knew about how the world works is in question

## The Fraying Near Millhaven (Sessions 1-3 Specifics)

The first Veilstone was destroyed in the Ashfall Peaks — far to the north. Millhaven is only experiencing the faintest edge of the effect. Symptoms are:

| Symptom | Location | Session |
|---------|----------|---------|
| Grain rot in the Greymere Mill — unnatural, spreading, resists treatment | The Mill | Session 1 |
| Shared nightmares among several townsfolk — visions of a pale light underground | Town-wide | Session 2 |
| A dead farmer's field blooming overnight with flowers that shouldn't exist in this climate | Farmstead south of town | Session 2-3 |
| The Old Stone Bridge humming at night — felt more than heard | South of town | Session 3 |
| Something moving in the Thornwood that the woodcutters refuse to talk about | Thornwood edge | Session 3 |

## GM Notes on Pacing

**The Fraying should feel like a slow tide, not a sudden flood.** Each session should have one or two things that are *slightly* more wrong than the last. The players should start by thinking "that's weird" and gradually shift to "something is very wrong" and finally to "the world is changing."

Let the players theorize. Let them be wrong. Let the NPCs disagree about what's happening. The mystery is the engine of Act 1.
