# ARNUM

Arnum sits a day's travel down the Arn River from Fallcrest, where the water broadens and slows enough to be forded and worked. Where Fallcrest guards the falls, Arnum works the calm below them: it is a town of ferrymen, millers, tanners, and traders who make their living moving goods along the river and the roads that meet at its banks. Most folk who live outside the town's low earthwork wall farm the rich bottomland or tend orchards on the surrounding rises; those inside the wall grind grain, cure hides, brew, or keep shop.

Where Fallcrest bends the knee to a hereditary Lord Warden, Arnum bends to no one. It is a free town, jealous of that freedom, governed by a council of its own guildmasters and elders. Caravans and river captains favor it precisely because no lord's levy waits at the gate and no banner flies above the council hall — a reputation the townsfolk guard as carefully as their strongboxes. *[DM: this independence is what earns Arnum its (Neutral) tag — it plays the Empire and the Empire's rivals against each other and commits to neither.]*

## Trade

Arnum trades with Fallcrest upriver, taking its timber, fruit, and grain and passing them downriver in exchange for finished goods, salt, and coin. Its own trades are milling, leatherwork, and river carriage: Arnum flour, cured hides, and boots travel far along the Arn, and its ferry and wharves make it the natural transfer point for anything moving between the upper vale and the lands below. What Fallcrest cannot use of its own leather, Arnum tans and finishes.

Because the town takes no lord's cut, its market prices run a shade lower than Fallcrest's, and its warehouses hold goods no one asks too many questions about. This makes Arnum a good place to buy, sell, and — quietly — to move things one would rather not declare.

## Getting In

Arnum has no true gate, only a break in its earthen wall where the river road enters, watched by the town wardens. There is no entry tax; the council learned long ago that a free crossing brings more custom than a tollman's purse. Wardens turn away only the obviously dangerous — the undead, the raving, the openly armed-for-war — and log caravans in a ledger at the Ferryhouse so the council knows who is within its bounds. Travelers who keep the peace and spend their coin are left entirely to their own business.

## ARNUM

**Population:** roughly 600 within the walls, with another 500 or so on the farms, orchards, and homesteads within a few miles. Mostly humans, with a strong minority of halflings along the wharves and a scattering of dwarves and half-elves among the artisans. *[DM: adjust racial mix to match Æthernia's demographics.]*

**Government:** No lord rules Arnum. A **Ferry Council** of seven — the heads of the millers', tanners', and boatmen's guilds, two elected elders, the harbormaster, and the keeper of the town ledger — meets in the Council Hall to set tolls, settle disputes, and hire the town's wardens. Its speaker holds no crown and serves at the council's pleasure. *[DM: name the current speaker and tie the council to your GOVERNMENTS faction file.]*

**Defense:** The **Arnum Wardens**, some thirty paid guards under a captain, keep the peace, watch the ferry and wharves, and man the earthwork wall. They are constables first and soldiers second. In real danger the council can raise perhaps 200 able townsfolk and river hands, but Arnum has always preferred to buy off, ferry away from, or talk down a threat rather than fight it.

## Arnum's Story

Arnum began as a ferry crossing and a mill — a place where the Arn ran slow enough to work and shallow enough to cross. Where Fallcrest rose under a Nerathan charter and a noble's tower, Arnum grew up around no lord at all, a knot of millers and boatmen who banded together to keep the crossing open and the road safe. When the empire's reach faded from the vale, Arnum lost less than its lordly neighbor: it had never leaned on a distant throne, and its council simply went on doing what it had always done. *[DM: reconcile "the empire" here with your Æthernia Empire faction and its timeline in 02 WORLD / HISTORY & TIMELINE.]*

The Bloodspear raids that gutted Fallcrest brushed Arnum but did not break it — the town paid, ferried, and endured, and some in Fallcrest have never quite forgiven Arnum for surviving so cheaply. In the lean decades since, Arnum has quietly become the busier of the two: the free crossing, the low prices, and the no-questions warehouses draw traffic that once would have stopped at the falls. That prosperity has drawn the eye of the Empire, which would dearly like a banner over the town's council hall — and of others who would rather it stayed free.

## Key Locations

**1. The Ferryhouse**
A long, timbered hall at the water's edge, half warehouse and half tollbooth, where the great flat ferry is winched across the Arn on chains. The harbormaster keeps the town ledger here, and every caravan and cargo passing through is logged in it. *[DM: the ledger is a natural adventure prop — a crossed-out entry, a cargo that was never claimed, a name that shouldn't be there.]*

**2. The Market Square**
An open, hard-packed square just inside the wall where river traders, farmers, and traveling merchants do business. Louder and cheaper than Fallcrest's Market Green, it is the best place in the upper vale to buy mundane goods, hear rumors, and find someone hiring.

**3. The Council Hall**
A plain stone building — deliberately unadorned, so no faction can claim it — where the Ferry Council meets. Petitioners, hirelings, and those with grievances wait on its benches. Adventurers looking for lawful work or a caravan contract start here.

**4. The Tanneries**
Downwind and downriver, where Arnum cures the hides that make its name. The tanners' guild is powerful, prickly, and secretive about its trade. *[DM: hook — something has been fouling the tannery water, or a shipment of hides came in wrong and no one will say from where.]*

**5. The Great Mill**
The waterwheel that started the town, still grinding Fallcrest's grain into Arnum's flour. The miller's family is old, respected, and quietly one of the richest households in town.

**6. The Drowned Sailor**
Arnum's largest inn and taproom, a boatmen's house on the wharves — cheap beds, strong drink, brawls, and the best river gossip for miles. Captains and porters trade news here nightly.

**7. The Silver Oar**
A quieter, dearer inn favored by merchants and travelers who want a clean room and no fistfights. Its keeper trades in discretion as much as lodging. *[DM: a good place to plant a contact, a fence, or an Empire agent traveling under a false name.]*

**8. The Warehouses**
A row of stout riverside stores where goods wait for the next boat or wagon. Some hold exactly what their manifests say. Some do not. *[DM: smuggling, a stolen cargo, or a "shipment" that is really someone hidden inside.]*

**9. The Shrine by the Water**
Arnum's temple is modest and, like the town, uncommitted — a shared house of worship with room for whatever gods its traders and river folk favor, tended by a handful of lay priests rather than a grand clergy. *[DM: choose which Æthernia deities are honored here from your RELIGION & COSMOLOGY / PANTHEONS files.]*

**10. The Wall & Warden Post**
Arnum's defense is a low earthwork bank topped with a timber palisade, more a boundary than a fortress, broken where the river road enters. The wardens keep a post at the break with a cell, an armory, and a bell to raise the town.

## DM Notes — Threads to Develop

- **The Empire's interest.** Arnum's independence is its whole identity. An Empire push to bring the free town "under protection" — by coin, by marriage into the council, or by force — is a ready campaign spine that puts the players' choices at the center. *[Tie to 03 FACTIONS & ORGANIZATIONS / GOVERNMENTS.]*
- **Fallcrest's resentment.** The two towns need each other and quietly dislike each other. Trade disputes, a diverted caravan, or a river blockade could set them at odds with the party in the middle.
- **The no-questions reputation.** Arnum's tolerance for undeclared cargo makes it a hub for smugglers, fences, and worse — good for low-level intrigue and a reason for outside powers to want it cleaned up (or kept dirty).
- **Named NPCs to create:** the Ferry Council speaker, the harbormaster/ledger-keeper, the tanners' guildmaster, the Great Mill's matriarch/patriarch, and the keeper of the Silver Oar.
