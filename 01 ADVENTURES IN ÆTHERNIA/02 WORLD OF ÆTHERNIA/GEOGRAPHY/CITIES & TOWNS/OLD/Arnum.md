# ARNUM

Arnum stands on the southern coast of the **Banner Sea**, the last built thing before the water that swallowed a civilization. It is the seat of **Cair Arnum**, and the oldest stones in it are older than the drowning — for Arnum began as **Ar‑Numath**, the remote fortress-harbor to which the disgraced **House Arn** was exiled in the years before the Sundering. That exile, meant as a living grave, became the ark: when the second sun fell and the heartland of Ar sank beneath the waves, the scholar-generals banished to Ar‑Numath were among the only Ar'an left alive. The town that grew from their harbor keeps their name, their long memory, and their old refusal to march to another's war.

Today Arnum is a **free city** — sworn to no empire, kneeling to no divine throne — which is both its pride and its danger. It sits at the meeting of trade roads and drowned sea-lanes: a place where salvage, secrets, and cargo all change hands, and where the powers of Æthernia press quietly at a town too useful to leave alone and too stubborn to be taken cleanly.

## The Heritage of House Arn

Where Ar was ruled by kings and banners, House Arn were **scholar-generals** — keepers of the **Codex Militaris**, loyal (as they always said) *to the nation, not the crown*. When **Lord Kaelen Arn** warned the Council of Banners that the coming war against Numinar was a manipulation and not a righteous cause, the house was branded traitor, stripped of the Codex, its kin executed, and its survivors shipped to Ar‑Numath to be forgotten. The Sundering proved Kaelen right and killed almost everyone who had condemned him.

That inheritance shapes Arnum to this day:
- A civic temperament of **wary neutrality** — the Arn were punished for reading a situation too clearly and refusing a flag; the city still trusts judgment over loyalty and treats every grand cause as someone else's trap. *[This is the in-world root of the "(Neutral)" tag.]*
- A surviving tradition of **scholarship and strategy** — Arnum keeps libraries, cartographers, and salvage-scholars where other ports keep only warehouses. *[DM: does a recovered fragment of the Codex Militaris survive here? Powerful hook.]*
- A quiet, unresolved question of **legitimacy** — if any true blood of the old realm of Ar endures, it endures in Arnum. That makes the city's leading families interesting to anyone who cares who "rightfully" rules a drowned kingdom.

## The Banner Sea at Its Door

Arnum's harbor opens onto a haunted water. The **Banner Sea** is the grave of Ar — the sunken capital of **Kar'ak** lies somewhere beneath it, and a faint ghost-light, said to be the **god's fire**, still burns far down in the dark. The ley lines here are corrupted, so spellcasting near the water runs unreliable; the currents defy the moon; drowned sailors have been seen rising to take ships; and mariners speak of the sea "collecting a debt" from any soul tied to Ar's fall. Three ships have vanished in the last decade, their logs later found on empty rafts, the final entries written in a hand no one recognizes.

For Arnum this means:
- **Salvage** is the city's most lucrative and most dangerous trade — divers and dredgers bring up relics of Ar (banner-fittings, Dral steel, temple-gold from drowned Numinar) and not all of it wants to be found.
- Most honest captains **won't fish or sail the deep Banner Sea**, so Arnum's fleet is small, its crews are strange, and its docks host the sort of people who *will* go out there.
- The sea itself is an **antagonist** — a slow supernatural pressure on the whole city, and a reason ghosts, curses, and the restless Ar'an dead surface in Arnum's stories.

## The Old Quarter & the Numinén Ruins

Beneath Arnum's **Old Quarter** lie the **Numinén ruins** — vaults and passages that predate the free city, remnants tied to fallen **Numinar** and the old Concord sea-lanes that once linked it to Ar. They are half-mapped, structurally treacherous, and (per *[[The Unraveling Ledger]]*) a place where the desperate go to hide and the criminal go to bury things. The Old Quarter above is the poorest and oldest district, and the ruins below make it the city's true underworld — literally.

## Trade & Getting In

Arnum takes no lord's levy at its gate — the free city learned that an open crossing draws more custom than a tollman's purse — so goods, and questions no one answers, move easily. It deals in salvage, cured goods, and river-and-road carriage between the interior of Cair Arnum and the wider South Blue. Its low duties and no-questions warehouses make it a natural hub for smugglers and fences as much as honest traders.

The city has no true wall on the seaward side, only its harbor and the water; its landward approaches are watched by the city's own wardens, who log caravans and turn away the openly dangerous but otherwise leave visitors to their business.

## The Powers Within

- **The Crimson Thread.** The slaving-and-assassination syndicate has sunk deep roots into Arnum. Its hierarchy — the **Weaver** at the top, the **Loom** as leadership, **Needles** and **Spindles** as operators, **Threads/Reds** as footsoldiers — runs behind merchant fronts and reaches into the council itself. *The Unraveling Ledger* turns on a stolen Thread ledger of ten years' bribes and corruption, an internal betrayal by the **Needle of the Cut**, and a terrified low-rank thief hiding in the Numinén ruins. Arnum is where that whole plot lives.
- **The Thieves Guild.** A rival underworld power that wants leverage over the Thread, not its destruction — because a vacuum in Arnum would be worse than the Thread itself. A useful counterweight and a potential (untrustworthy) ally for player characters.
- **The leading houses / free council.** The legitimate authority, proud of the city's independence, compromised in places, and courted from outside by the **Empire of Æthernia / Divine Empire** who would love a banner over Arnum's council hall at last.

## Key Locations

**1. The Harbor & Salvage Quays**
The working heart of Arnum, where dredgers and divers land what the Banner Sea gives up. The best relics — and the worst curses — come ashore here.

**2. The Old Quarter**
The oldest, poorest district, riddled below with the **Numinén ruins**. The city's underworld in every sense; the *Unraveling Ledger* plot hides here.

**3. The Numinén Ruins (undercity)**
Half-mapped vaults beneath the Old Quarter — hideouts, dumping grounds, and older, darker things tied to fallen Numinar. A ready-made dungeon. *[DM: what did the Concord leave down here that no one has found yet?]*

**4. The Arn Archive**
The scholar-generals' inheritance: a library and salvage-scholars' hall where the city keeps maps, recovered Ar'an relics, and its long memory. *[DM: possible resting place of a Codex Militaris fragment.]*

**5. The Council Hall**
Seat of the free city's leading houses and guilds. Deliberately unostentatious — no lord's banner flies — but the Crimson Thread has friends inside its walls.

**6. The Warehouses**
Riverside and dockside stores where undeclared cargo waits for the next tide. Some hold what their manifests say; some hold people who would rather not be found.

**7. A Sailors' Taphouse** *(name TBD — e.g., "The Drowned Banner")*
The loud harbor-house where the strange crews who *will* sail the Banner Sea drink, boast, and trade the only reliable news of what's happening out on the water.

**8. The Shrine of the Uncommitted**
Arnum's temple is modest and, like the city, sworn to no single power — a shared house of worship for whatever gods its traders and salvagers favor, tended by lay priests. *[DM: choose honored deities from your PANTHEONS files; note the ley-corruption near the sea may make divine magic here uneasy.]*

**9. The Landward Warden Post**
The city's only real gate, where the trade road meets the town. Wardens log caravans, keep a bell to raise the city, and otherwise leave visitors alone.
