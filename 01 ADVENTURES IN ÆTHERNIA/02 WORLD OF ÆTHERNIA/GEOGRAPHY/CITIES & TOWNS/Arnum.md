# Ar'num
*The City That Remains — Seat of the Wardens of the Remnant*

![[BannerAndShield.png]]

**Also Known As:** Arnum, New Ar, the City on the Cliffs, the Harbor of the Shield
**Region:** [[South Blue]], coast opposite the [[Banner Sea]]
**Government:** City-state ruled by the **Wardens of the Remnant** ([[House Arn]])
**Population:** Ar'an, Numinarian, and mixed descent; sizable foreign harbor quarter
**Sigil:** A banner standing within a curved shield of light

Ar'num is the last living piece of the drowned nation of [[Ar]]. It is a cliff-terraced harbor city on a foreign coast far from the heartland its founders once called home, and it exists only because two disgraces — one martial, one priestly — were sent there to be forgotten and instead saved a civilization.

Its people are the descendants of exiles and refugees. They live by a sea that swallowed their ancestors and rule themselves by a doctrine every child of the city learns before their tenth year: *trust no oracle that has not been questioned twice.*

___
## I. Ar-Numath — The Grave Meant for House Arn

Long before the Sundering, when [[Ar]] and [[Numinar]] were still bound by the **Concord of the Two Pillars**, the Ar'an Council of Banners built a small garrison-harbor on a foreign coast to guard the far end of the old Concord sea-lanes. They called it **Ar-Numath** — a workmanlike name meaning "Ar's Watch" — and for a century or two it did what it was built for. Then trade routes shifted, the Concord aged into complacency, and the Council let the outpost slide out of memory. By the time the [[War in Heaven]] came, Ar-Numath was a salt-bitten collection of cracked ramparts, a half-silted harbor, and a garrison of aging soldiers whose families had long since stopped receiving new banners from home.

It was into this dying outpost that [[House Arn]] was cast.

When [[Lord Kaelen Arn]] rose before the Council of Banners and named the war for what it was — the work of the Deceiver, not of mortal enmity — his house was branded craven, then heretical, then treasonous. The banner-halls of House Arn were burned in the streets while the city watch stood aside. Three of Kaelen's kin were executed on fabricated charges of Numinarian collaboration. The survivors were stripped of their titles, denied even the courtesy of a war-banner, and marched under guard to the coast. There they were loaded onto a single old galley and shipped to Ar-Numath, out of sight of the nation they were said to have betrayed.

The Council intended it as a living grave. In the private minutes of that session — later recovered by Ar'num's scholar-generals — the phrase used was *"let them rot at the edge of the world."*

Ar-Numath was chosen precisely because no one cared what happened there.

## II. The Second Exile — Oreleth of the Old Gods

In the same years — for the Deceiver's war provoked mirror-treasons in Numinar as it did in Ar — a **blind** Numinarian priest named **Oreleth** stood in the great sanctuary at the heart of Numinar and denounced the war-omens as false. Oreleth had been sightless since childhood, and for years the oracle-hierarchy had suffered the blind priest as a harmless curiosity — until the day the sightless one alone named the omens for the lies that all the seeing eyes had missed. For this Oreleth was stripped of rank, stripped of name-rites, and cast out of the temple-cities. Where House Arn had drifted to Ar-Numath by the Council's decree, Oreleth drifted there by the older logic of exile: the outpost was the one place along the old Concord lanes that would take a disgraced heretic in.

For years before the Fall, the two disgraces lived side by side in the crumbling harbor. Oreleth kept a small shrine on the mole where the sea-wall met the quay, dedicated to the **old gods** — the elder powers of the heavens' first order, long since abandoned by Numinar's oracle-hierarchy as obsolete. The Ar'an exiles did not worship there, but neither did they interfere. In a place meant for forgetting, both disgraces had already been forgotten by everything except each other.

This companionship of the disgraced would matter more than either exile ever suspected.

## III. The Sundering and the Shield Prayer

When the [[Sundering]] came, Ar-Numath was more than a thousand miles from the capital of Ar. The people of the outpost saw the sky flash white at midday — a light so bright it cast shadows away from itself — and then, hours later, they heard a rolling roar from the western horizon, like the sky tearing loose from its hinges. Refugees would later tell them what they had seen: a column of fire climbing into the heavens above where the capital had been, spreading at its crown like a burning tree.

They did not understand yet. Then the sea began to draw back.

The wave that came for Ar-Numath had been born of the drowning heartland. It had crossed an ocean to arrive. The horizon simply rose — a moving range of black water taller than the fortress itself, crowned with the wreckage of the old trade fleets it had already taken. No sea-wall in the world could have stood against it. The garrison began the evacuation drill they all knew was pointless.

Blind Oreleth walked out alone onto the sea-wall — needing no eyes, the survivors would say, to face a darkness that was coming for them all.

What Oreleth spoke was the **Shield Prayer**, a rite of the old gods so ancient that the temple-cities of Numinar had preserved it only as a scholarly curiosity — a prayer from the age when the heavens still answered plainly. The accounts of what followed agree in every important particular: light leapt up from the water's edge in a great curved wall, like a tower shield braced across the mouth of the bay, and the wave *broke against it*. Three times the sea struck, and three times the shield held, ringing (witnesses said) like a struck blade. Grey ash fell on the ramparts like snow. Above the harbor the light burned on until the waters lay flat.

When the waters lay flat again, no two accounts agree on what became of the blind priest. Some swear Oreleth was found kneeling on the sea-wall, alive but hollowed and spent past speaking, and lived on nameless among the exiles for years after. Others say the sea-wall stood bare, the little shrine untouched, and Oreleth was never seen in the harbor again — that the old gods, or the sea, or the prayer itself had claimed the price a miracle demands. The city has never settled which is true, and by long custom does not try. It keeps only the three words the sightless priest spoke as the shield held and he fell: *a fair trade.*

Whether the old gods answered because some of them had survived the [[War in Heaven]], because the prayer spent the last of their lingering power, or because it was the dying strength of the very god that fell on Ar — turned at the last to shelter rather than ruin — is the central question of Ar'num's theology, and it has never been resolved. The city does not require an answer. It requires only that the shrine be tended.

## IV. The Mercy of Arn and the Founding of Ar'num

When the sky cleared, the exiles of Ar-Numath understood what none of them had expected to understand: the outpost meant to be their grave was now the largest surviving piece of Ar'an civilization in the world.

In the seasons that followed, the refugees came. Not overland — the heartland roads now ran into the sea — but by ship. Broken banner-companies aboard commandeered war-galleys. Ar'an farmers in fishing boats never meant for open water. And, most remarkable of all, whole flotillas of Numinarian survivors, whose oracles had been unmasked and whose temples had fallen with the sky, and who found in the one house of Ar that had opposed the war the only Ar'ans they could bring themselves to trust.

Lord Kaelen Arn, now old, ordered the harbor-chains lowered for every sail on the horizon and issued the decree remembered forever after as the **Mercy of Arn**:

> *"The Deceiver made us enemies. We will not do his work for him a day longer."*

The city that grew up from the harbor and climbed the cliffs took a new name. Officially, **Ar'num** meant "New Ar" in the old tongue. But the second meaning was the one that mattered, and every child in the city learns to hear it: *Ar and Num* — the two peoples made one.

Foreign traders, unable to manage the glottal stop in the old tongue, softened the name to **Arnum** on their ledgers and bills of lading. Both spellings are correct. The people of the city write it *Ar'num* on their own documents and answer to *Arnum* everywhere else without complaint.

## V. Ar'num Today

Ar'num climbs its cliffs in terraces above the harbor, its skyline a marriage of Ar'an banner-towers and Numinarian temple-domes rising over a forest of masts. The people who once ruled the land as its sword-arm now live by the sea that drowned their nation, and they have made themselves masters of it. The **Iron Banners** endure — but they march up gangplanks now, and the city's marine banner-companies are the most feared soldiers afloat in the [[South Blue]].

The **Banner System** endures as well. Every citizen of Ar'num, from harbor rat to Warden's heir, still serves their season under a banner. To lose one's banner in service is still the deepest shame the city recognizes; to abandon it, still worse than death. But the reconstructed **Codex Militaris** now opens not with Aruk the Unbroken's ancient axiom on necessary war, but with Kaelen Arn's addendum, added the year of the Mercy:

> *"The first duty of the soldier is to know who aims the spear."*

The martial arts of old Ar are preserved alongside the surviving rites of Numinar. The city's priests hold a doctrine unique among the nations: all oracles, omens, prophecies, and divine messages are to be received with formal suspicion, examined by both priest and scholar-general before being acted upon. This doctrine is called the **Twice-Questioned Word**, and it is taught in every temple and every banner-hall in the city. The Ar'ans of Ar'num do not trust the heavens. They remember why.

There is one temple in the city where the Twice-Questioned Word does not apply: **Oreleth's Shrine**, where the sea-wall meets the quay. The old gods answered when the city called, and the city does not forget its debts. By ancient charter, the shrine is tended jointly by a priest of Numinar and a scholar-general of Ar — the same pairing, in miniature, that saved the city.

## VI. The Wardens of the Remnant

**[[House Arn]]** rules still, the last direct bloodline of old Ar's nobility. Its lords do not take the title of king — the throne of Ar is held to have died with it, and to claim otherwise would be to insult the drowned — but style themselves **Wardens of the Remnant**. The Warden's authority is real but explicitly custodial: they hold the city in trust for a nation that no longer exists, and every Warden's investiture includes the formal recitation of that fact.

The city's sigil is a banner standing within a curved shield of light — House Arn's defiance and Oreleth's prayer, the two disgraces that saved a civilization. It is stitched, embossed, carved, and painted onto nearly every surface of civic importance in Ar'num.

By ancient custom the reigning Warden keeps two objects on the wall of the council hall:

- The **scorched banner of House Arn** — smuggled out of the burned hall in the old capital during the exile, and thus the only great banner of old Ar that does not lie at the bottom of the [[Banner Sea|Drowned Banner Sea]].
- Beneath it, an **empty frame**, which holds the burned-out name of the **Deceiver**.

The frame is empty so that no one forgets, and the name is burned so that no one calls him back.

## VII. The Rite of Ten Thousand Banners

Once a year, on the anniversary of the Sundering, ships sail from Ar'num to the still waters above the drowned capital. No anchor is dropped — nothing of the living is permitted to touch the dead nation — and the descendants of Ar and Numinar together lower ten thousand paper banners onto the waves, one for every grave beneath them.

A priest, blindfolded in memory of the blind Oreleth, speaks the opening words of the Shield Prayer — never the whole of it, for the whole of it belongs to Oreleth alone. The ships turn for home in silence, and no one looks back at the water until the light below has passed from sight.

Sailors of other nations do not accompany the fleet. It is not that they are forbidden. It is that the city has never had to explain why they should not come.

It should not have survived.
