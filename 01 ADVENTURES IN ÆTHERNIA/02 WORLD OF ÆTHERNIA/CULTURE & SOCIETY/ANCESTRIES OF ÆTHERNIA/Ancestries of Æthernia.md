>*"Ask a priest what a soul is and he will tell you it is the gods' gift to us. Ask a philosopher and he will tell you it is our gift to the gods. I have come to suspect that the difference between these two answers is the whole of history."* - Aldric Dunstan, A Guide to Æthernian History I

When the gods created Æthernia, they imbued the chosen amongst their creation with the right to be made in their own image, possessing of a soul. This soul, and the clarity it provides, is what grants the **Kith and Kin** dominion over the world.

Every ancestry that holds a soul is called **soulblessed**. Every ancestry that does not is called a great many other things, most of them ugly, and the distinction between the two has been used to justify nearly every atrocity in the historical record.

## The Breath and the Making

The oldest account, and the only one all the pantheons agree upon, holds that in the first year of the Mythic Age the Great Dragon [[Tiān]] burst forth from the Mysts and breathed Encara into existence. What Tiān made was a world: stone and sea, beast and green growing thing, complete and turning and utterly indifferent.

It was not enough.

The gods who followed — the first-born of the Mysts, the elder powers of the first order — found themselves sovereigns of a creation that could not know they existed. A mountain cannot kneel. A wolf cannot be grateful. And so the gods went back into the clay a second time and made something new: creatures shaped in their own likeness, given the spark that lets a thing look at the sky and wonder who put it there.

The priests call this the Second Making, and teach it as the supreme act of divine love.

The historians have begun, very quietly, to teach it as something else.

## The Ambrosia Problem

The gods were born of the Myst and were mighty in their own right, but somewhere in the long ages between the Making and the War in Heaven, their strength came to rest on a different foundation entirely. Worship became the true source of their power — one prayer, one offering, one act of faith at a time. A god of a thousand temples was a fortress. A god whose faithful lay slaughtered was a beggar.

This is the uncomfortable conclusion that has gotten more than one scholar excommunicated: that souls were not given to the Kith and Kin as a gift, but installed in them as a function. A soul is the organ by which a mortal produces worship. The clarity it provides — the reason, the memory, the capacity to name a god and mean it — is precisely the machinery required to sustain a divine being.

By this reading, the soulblessed are not the beloved children of the gods. They are the harvest.

Adherents of the older orthodoxy answer that a thing can be both, that a parent may love a child and still depend upon them in old age, and that only a cynic insists the two cannot coexist. The argument is very old and has never been settled. It is worth noting only that in the years since the War in Heaven, the second reading has quietly gained ground, because the War proved beyond any doubt that gods can starve.

## Kith and Kin

The paired term is far older than any nation now standing, and it does not mean what most people assume.

**Kin** are the humans — the youngest of the soulblessed, the shortest-lived, and by a wide margin the most numerous. They spread, they build, they breed, and they bury their dead at a rate the other ancestries find genuinely alarming to watch.

**Kith** are all the rest of the soulblessed elders: the [[Edowan (Elf)|Edowan]], the [[JàrnFòlk (Dwarf)|JàrnFòlk]], the [[Tuath Dé (Gnome)|Tuath Dé]], the [[(Halfling)|halflings]], and by their own insistence the [[01 ADVENTURES IN ÆTHERNIA/02 WORLD OF ÆTHERNIA/CULTURE & SOCIETY/ANCESTRIES OF ÆTHERNIA/Dragonborn|dragonborn]]. What binds them is not blood but time. A Kith ancestry measures a life in centuries and a grudge in generations, and its institutions are built on the assumption that the people who founded them will still be present to see them fail.

For centuries good and evil were held in balance by the just rule of the Kingdoms of Kith and Kin, and the phrase survives from that age as a legal formula more than a poetic one. To be *of Kith and Kin* is to be a person under the law. It is the wording used in treaties, in charters, in writs of protection, and — most consequentially — in the statutes that make the enslavement of the soulblessed a hanging crime in a dozen realms while leaving the soulless entirely unprotected. Organisations such as [[The Crimson Thread]] and the so-called League of Ethical Slavers have built their entire commercial and moral case on exactly where that line is drawn, and have spilled a great deal of ink arguing that it should be drawn somewhere else.

The gods themselves were never especially reverent about it. As Dunstan observed, they had long used both Kith and Kin to settle their own disputes, reserving the ultimate powers of the Myst to themselves and spending mortal armies freely on quarrels that mortals were never told the shape of. In 0010 Œ, near the end of the War in Heaven, the gods conspired with Kith and Kin to devise a bomb filled with the magical energies of Encara and destroy the Demon Dragon Neindan. It worked. What it cost is recorded elsewhere, and the [[Banner Sea]] is still there to be sailed over.

## The Ancestries Without a Charter

Not every soulblessed people can point to a god who claims them.

The [[Klaxon (Half-Orc)|Klaxon]], the Kithkin ([[(Half-Elf)|half-elves]]), and the Neindari ([[01 ADVENTURES IN ÆTHERNIA/02 WORLD OF ÆTHERNIA/CULTURE & SOCIETY/ANCESTRIES OF ÆTHERNIA/Tiefling|tieflings]]) all carry souls that no doctrine accounts for and no pantheon ever announced. Each arrived in the world sideways — through unions the theologians had not anticipated, or through the wreckage the gods left behind them — and each has spent its entire existence being told, in one form or another, that it is a clerical error.

This is precisely the problem they represent. If a soul is a gift the gods bestow, then a soul appearing where no god bestowed it means either that a god acted in secret, or that souls can arise without the gods at all. The temples have never produced a satisfying answer to this and have generally preferred not to be asked. The scattered peoples themselves have their own view of the matter, and it is not a charitable one.

## The Soulless

Orc-kind, goblins, kobolds and their like are held by every established faith to be soulless: made things, or unmade things, animate but unblessed, incapable of true worship and therefore of no use and no interest to the gods.

It should be recorded that this is doctrine, not observation. It should also be recorded that the doctrine is enormously profitable, and that the ancestries it excludes have never been invited to comment.
