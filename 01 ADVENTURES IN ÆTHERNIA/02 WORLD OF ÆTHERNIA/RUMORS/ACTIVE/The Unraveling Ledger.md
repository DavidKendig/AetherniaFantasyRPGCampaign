# The Unraveling Ledger

## The Rumor as Heard

A low-ranking member of [[The Crimson Thread]] — a Red, or possibly a low Thread — has stolen a ledger from one of the Spindles operating in or near [[Arnum]]. The ledger is said to contain the names of nobles, guild masters, temple officials, and wealthy merchants who have secretly funded, sheltered, or done business with the Crimson Thread over the last decade.

The thief has gone to ground. The Crimson Thread is tearing the city apart trying to find him before the book reaches the wrong hands. The [[Thieves Guild]] is *also* looking for him — not to help, but to buy.

Every day the ledger is missing, the streets get more dangerous. Doors are being kicked in. Familiar faces are disappearing. A [[Moondrip]] den on the docks was burned to ash last week and no one has claimed responsibility.

___
## Where the PCs Might Hear It

- A drunk longshoreman at a dockside tavern (roll for accuracy — he'll swear the thief was his cousin)
- A city guard captain who has quietly been told to *not* investigate the recent murders in the merchant quarter
- A worried temple almoner whose superior has been meeting with strange men after dark
- A frightened Red who thinks they're going to be Frayed next and wants to disappear
- A Thieves Guild fence, who will hint that a *very valuable book* is coming on the market and asks if the PCs know anyone in the market for leverage

___
## Gather Information

**DC 10:** *"Yeah, something's got the Thread spooked. They're pulling in favors from every hole they own. Don't take contracts near the wharf right now — the wrong wrong-place-wrong-time will get you dead."*

**DC 15:** The disturbance began roughly two tendays ago after an unannounced meeting between a Spindle and someone flying colors no one recognized. Since then, the Spindle in question has not been seen in public. Rumor suggests he is either dead, hiding, or has been Frayed.

**DC 20:** The stolen item is a ledger — leather-bound, brass-cornered, sealed with red wax and thread. It records ten years of "tithe returns" from the [[Needle of the Gathering]]'s territory: which nobles took Crimson coin, which temples looked the other way for a cut, which merchants bought slaves from [[Needle of the Binding|the Binding]] under paper contracts. If it ever reaches a magistrate — or a rival power — it collapses a decade of leverage in a night.

**DC 25:** The thief is not a disillusioned true believer. He was placed. Someone inside [[The Loom]] itself — a mid-tier intelligence handler — turned him, briefed him, and handed him the Spindle's schedule. That handler is now missing as well. Whoever pulled this off is *inside the Thread* and playing a much longer game than a single ledger's worth of extortion.

___
## The Truth (DM Only)

The ledger is real. The thief is real. He is currently hiding in a Numinén ruin beneath the Old Quarter, alone, terrified, and rapidly running out of food and water. He believes he is a hero of The Rising, that he is going to expose the corruption of the Inner Circle to the true believers and spark a genuine uprising against the leadership.

He is wrong about almost everything.

The theft was orchestrated by the **[[Needle of the Cut]]**, who intends to use the fallout to eliminate a rival Needle (whose Spindle owned the ledger), curry favor with **The Weaver**, and quietly murder the thief the moment the ledger changes hands. The handler who recruited the thief is already dead — his body sunk in a barrel of salted brine somewhere in the Banner Sea shallows.

The **Thieves Guild** knows enough to smell blood. They do not want the ledger to *destroy* the Crimson Thread — a vacuum is worse than a rival — but they want a *copy* of every page. Enough to make sure the Thread never operates freely in Arnum again.

**The Weaver** does not yet know the Needle of the Cut is behind this. If he finds out before the Needle finishes, there will be an internal war that spills into the streets. If he finds out after, the Needle of the Cut may become the next Weaver.

___
## Hooks and Complications

- **Someone hires the party to find the thief.** Who? The Crimson Thread (posing as merchants), the Thieves Guild (openly), a noble whose name is in the ledger (desperately), or a magistrate who has heard whispers (quietly).
- **Someone tries to kill the party for asking questions.** The Needle of the Cut's assassins do not care who else dies to keep this quiet.
- **A PC's contact vanishes.** A friendly NPC — a guard, a shopkeeper, a temple friend — is dragged out of their home at night. Their name may or may not be in the ledger. The PCs will not know until it's too late to save them.
- **The thief tries to recruit the PCs.** He genuinely thinks he is the hero of this story. If the PCs get to him first, he will beg them to help him bring the book to a "true friend of the people" — which could be anyone from a firebrand cleric to a rival crime lord depending on his contacts.
- **The ledger is a McGuffin the party can never quite hold.** If the PCs take possession of it, *every* faction in the city will move against them. Delivering it, destroying it, copying it, or hiding it are all campaign-shaping choices.

___
## Escalation Timeline

If the PCs do not intervene:

- **Week 1:** Disappearances continue. A minor noble is found dead in an alley, throat cut, red thread stitched into his lips.
- **Week 2:** The thief is found. The ledger is returned. Half a dozen names in it are quietly murdered over the following month to eliminate loose ends.
- **Week 3:** The Needle of the Cut moves against the rival Needle. Bodies pile up in the harbor.
- **Week 4:** The Weaver reasserts control. The city is quieter than it has been in years — which is worse, because everyone left standing is loyal.
